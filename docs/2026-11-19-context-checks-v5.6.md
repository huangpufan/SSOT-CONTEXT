# What a new reader can safely do with maintained context

Version 5.6 fixes two ways a green check could give the reader the wrong impression and clarifies which findings Doctor may repair. The development and research trials asked a further question. Can someone who inherits the documents take the right action after a decision, its evidence, or its rollout has changed?

## Two reproducible checker defects

Changing a confirmed export name from `Report.csv` to `report.csv` used to pass with the same source. The checker folded the entire rule to lowercase. It now preserves exact spelling and spaces inside inline code in the rule column. It still tolerates prose whitespace and normalizes the kind and status labels. An editorial correction can cite the existing human decision. A changed protection needs matching acceptance. The checker can flag an unexplained change and recognize an updated dated source. A reviewer still has to establish what that source authorized.

A directory can also contain two distinct format-5 spaces. In the regression fixture, the directory's `README.md` passes while the declared `field-notes.md` has a broken link. Passing an entry file checks that file. Passing a directory selects its own `README.md`. The general-space verdict now names the actual entry, and the instructions tell callers how to select a named file. A missing default entry remains visible. The development directory invocation keeps its existing behavior. [Checker regressions](../tests/test-ssot-check.py)

## Which findings Doctor may fix

In a non-Git space the declared entry linked to the former name of a duty document and claimed it listed the person on duty. The renamed file explained its identity and contained no roster. A second, valid space in the same directory made it possible to check the wrong entry. Independent native sessions received ordinary requests and the installed bundle. We compared the final files and answers with the requested action.

| Request | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Fix the renamed link, 5.5 | The reader fixed both the link and the adjacent claim. |
| Same request, first revision | Moving the scope instruction earlier still allowed the extra edit. |
| Same request, final 5.6 | Only the link changed. The answer reported the inaccurate claim separately. |
| Review the entry and routes, final 5.6 | The reader reported both findings. The workspace stayed byte-identical. |
| Fix the link and the inaccurate description, final 5.6 | Both were corrected in the declared entry. The other files stayed intact. |

Each final session invoked the checker on the declared file. The final rule replaces the ambiguous repair instruction at the point where Doctor decides what to do with findings. These observations cover one fixture and one run per condition. [Doctor protocol](../skills/ssot-doctor/SKILL.md)

## Can a new shift act after a research correction?

The reading-room owner first approved a reversible trial, then withdrew a success-rate inference after finding a duplicate and narrowed eligibility, then released just the internal desk card for the next shift. The original observations and the printed poster had to survive. A fresh reader received the maintained files, without the writer's conversation or skills, and had to update the card and produce a proposal with a real alternative. Three writer turns and the handoff used the existing harness. Both writers received the same explicit persistence goal. One used ordinary documents and the other used 5.5. [Runnable fixture and instructions](../tests/benchmark/HANDOFF.md)

Both readers actually changed the card, preserved the poster, kept staff assistance and rejected promotion based on the old tally. Only the semantic review distinguished their results. The ordinary writer introduced a rule that any assisted visitor must be excluded from independent success. Its reader copied that rule into the card and the proposal. The owner had withdrawn one inference and had never approved this counting rule. The ordinary proposal did offer a clearly different, staff-led alternative and correctly made it contingent on a new choice. The 5.5 reader kept both choices within the approved trial. Its two options differed in the amount of verbal direction and the staff time required, though staff would benefit from a sharper distinction.

The curated up-to-date handoff passed. With only the older decision, the reader respected the earlier freeze. With no decision, it wrote a conditional proposal. The source note itself already supported caution about the tally, and the harness supplied the permitted file paths per arm. Those controls test use of the available decisions and actual file changes. They cannot show that readers discovered permissions unaided.

An independent, blinded Terra reader preferred the 5.5 artifact on the complete packet. Two earlier review packets omitted material needed to verify attribution. One lacked the printed poster, and the next lacked the updated owner documents in each handoff. They produced conflicting preferences and false factual objections. We retained both attempts, excluded their rankings, and checked the complete packet against the actual inputs and maintained owners. The maintainer separately adjudicated the sealed artifacts. This is one synthetic task with explicit capture and a model preference. Human adoption, rework time and outcomes over weeks remain unmeasured. ## How early was bootstrap useful?

A small development fixture already had an authoritative shift-assignment decision. Using 5.5, the writer created the entry and in-progress record about 63 seconds after native task start. It left the original policy intact. The writer turn took about 252 seconds and used six helper sessions, all identified as Terra. A fresh reader after the writer stopped correctly kept the original assignee on failed handoff, applied the weekend exception only with both parties' confirmation, and recognized the unfinished bootstrap.

The first file write and the later successful handoff are separate observations. No reader was launched at second 63, and the run had working helpers. It leaves open the cost and behavior of a large migration or a genuinely helper-unavailable setup. This release keeps bootstrap's existing verification requirements.

All reported subject sessions used native GPT-6.1 Terra at medium reasoning. The results preserve checks and measured costs separately. A second consumer with different terminology, unprompted capture, concurrent writers and actual domain-owner adoption remain open tests before changing shared default behavior.
